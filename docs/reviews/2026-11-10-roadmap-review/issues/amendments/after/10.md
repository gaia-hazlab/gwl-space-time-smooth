## Revised scope — approved by the PI, 2026-10-08

**In plain words:** Fix sea level at the shoreline only; score near-coast wells.

**Audit verdict:** rewrite

### Specification

Rewrite as: impose head=MSL (NAVD88 offset, VDatum) on shoreline cells only, with a Dupuit/Ghyben-Herzberg inland profile as a soft prior; add a valley/riparian groundwater sink in water_budget (coordinate with #175); acceptance on held-out near-coast wells (bias and RMSE versus distance from shore), plus fraction of DTW<0 cells outside NWI wetlands/floodplain. Add BLOCKED_BY 10:[21].

### Problems found

- The acceptance 'coastal cells converge to sea level ± tidal range' is physically and quantitatively wrong.
- Targets only legacy Stage-1/3 files, not the water budget that drives the twin.
- Depends on the domain raster (#21) and the dist_coast raster (DataHub fetch_gaia dist_coast).
- 'DTW<0 only in floodplain/tidal (<0.1%)' conflicts with real valley-floor wetlands and artesian wells, which upstream QC currently deletes.

### Physics assessment

Partly wrong physics. The water table equals mean sea level only AT the shoreline. Inland it rises (Dupuit/Ghyben-Herzberg; tidal forcing also raises the mean inland head, cf. Nielsen 1990). A 2 km / HAND<=10 m 'coastal' cell therefore should NOT 'converge to sea level ± tidal range'. Puget Sound's ~3-4.5 m tidal range also makes that band nearly untestable. HAND already references the nearest drainage, so a HAND-based prior implicitly carries a stream-stage boundary, and sparse gauge stage adds little. The real defect is in the water budget: valley cells (HAND<=d0) have no groundwater sink (water_budget.py:193-196). No coastal or stage code exists anywhere in src/.

## Context
The field is interpolated without explicit hydrologic boundary conditions.

## Hydrologist rationale
The wells *see* boundary conditions the model must honor: at the shoreline the water table is
pinned near **sea level** (Puget Sound / coastal aquifers), and along streams the water table is
coupled to **stream stage**. Ignoring them lets the interpolant drift to non-physical heads near
the coast and rivers — exactly the low-lying, liquefaction-prone, road-bearing corridors.

## Tasks
- [ ] Impose a coastal Dirichlet-style constraint: head → mean sea level (with tidal/SLR offset)
      at the `coastal` boundary; do **not** smooth across it.
- [ ] Couple valley-floor cells to stream stage (NHD + gauge/stage where available) as a soft prior.
- [ ] Verify no over-land water table (DTW < 0) outside active floodplains/tidal zones.

## Acceptance
- Coastal cells converge to sea level ± tidal range; stream-adjacent cells track stage; DTW<0
  only in floodplain/tidal cells (<0.1%).

## Downstream relevance
- **Geotech/DOT:** coastal and river corridors carry the most road infrastructure and the
  highest liquefaction + lateral-spread + embankment-failure exposure.

Affected: `src/models/interpolate_residuals.py`, `src/models/baseline_regression.py`, paper §Stage 3.
