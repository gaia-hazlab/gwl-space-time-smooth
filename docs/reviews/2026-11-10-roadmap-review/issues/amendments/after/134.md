### Physics assessment

c_r and colluvium thickness H are necessary FS inputs. Literature priors exist: c_r about 2-25 kPa in PNW conifer forests and about 1-7 kPa in clearcuts (Schmidt et al. 2001); H from soil-production/slope models, or SOLUS depth. Note that SOLUS is capped at 2 m (#224 caveat), which matters for anything deeper. The post-fire acceptance is physically wrong on timing and mechanism. (1) Root reinforcement does not drop at the fire and recover over 3 yr. Roots of killed trees decay over years while regrowth lags, so c_r reaches its MINIMUM about 3-10+ years after stand death (Sidle 1992 decay/regrowth model). (2) The 1-3 yr post-fire debris-flow hazard is dominantly RUNOFF-generated (reduced infiltration, sediment bulking; USGS Staley et al. 2017 M1 model with I15, dNBR and soil erodibility), not infinite-slope shallow failure. Reduced Ks actually lowers infiltration and pore pressure, which can RAISE FS in year 1. So 'a burn perimeter lowers FS and recovers over ~3 yr' tests the wrong hazard model.
