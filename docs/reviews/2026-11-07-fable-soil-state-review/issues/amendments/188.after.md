### Physics assessment

LETKF/EnKF over storage states, with native-support operators and localization by hydrographic or hydrogeologic connectivity, is the right next estimator, and doing hydrology first is right. Physics gaps in the body. (1) 'Enforce state bounds' by clipping members creates or destroys water and biases the mean. Use a bounded transform (anamorphosis/logit) or book the clipped mass in the DA-increment ledger as a non-physical flux. (2) A bucket or water-budget model is strongly dissipative, so forcing perturbations alone will collapse the spread. Parameter perturbation and Q (from #192) are needed from day one, not later. (3) Temporally correlated observation errors (dv/v Cd with a 3-day correlation; satellite retrieval bias) violate the white-noise assumption. (4) 'Invariant to observation ordering' holds for LETKF but only approximately for a serial EnSRF with localization; state the tolerance. (5) The flagship notebook make_twin_gif.py:248-252 still updates frames independently and injects synthetic observations (science review :85).

## Why this is needed

The repository now has a sound static observation-space BLUE, correct OU gain/drift treatment for lagged data, native-support operators, and a Matérn prior with hydrographic region masking. The wet-season twin still applies independent BLUE corrections to frames on a deterministic trajectory, discards posterior covariance, and restarts from a prescribed prior at the next frame.

That is not a Kalman nowcast. The analysis uncertainty never becomes the next forecast's initial uncertainty, and model/process uncertainty is not propagated.

## Dependencies

- Depends on #187 for the ensemble/state contract.
- Builds on the partial fixes in #161 (OU lag treatment is landed; dynamic propagation remains) and #163 (Matérn + region masking landed; scalable representation remains).
- Depends on #154 for a production-scale strategy; a localized ensemble pilot may proceed before a full SPDE/GMRF implementation.
- Uses #164/#165/#168 for defensible observation errors/operators.
- Uses #155 for deterministic ensemble random streams and #156 for fixture-backed assimilation tests.
- Can prototype on the current calibrated daily budget, but release depends on #171/#172/#137.
- Supersedes the independent-frame update pattern in `notebooks/make_twin_gif.py`; coordinate with #162 before augmenting with damage/disturbance states.

## Proposed method

Implement a localized EnKF/LETKF cycle over storage-based hydrologic states:

`x[t+1,f,e] = M(x[t,a,e], u[t,e], p[e]) + eta[t,e]`

followed by an observation-space analysis at each cycle. Start with hydrology only; add mechanical memory after stability and calibration are demonstrated.

## Tasks

- [ ] Define forecast, observation, localization, inflation, constraint, and persistence interfaces.
- [ ] Propagate every ensemble member through the daily water-budget/snow model.
- [ ] Use observation operators at native spatial and depth support.
- [ ] Carry the analysis ensemble and covariance into the next forecast cycle.
- [ ] Localize by distance plus hydrographic/hydrogeologic connectivity; prevent leakage across divides.
- [ ] Enforce state bounds and recompute diagnostic variables from corrected storage.
- [ ] Record per-member and ensemble-mean DA storage increments separately from physical fluxes.
- [ ] Retain the static BLUE as a tested limiting/reference case.
- [ ] Add a small fixture-based cycling experiment before scaling to the 2.96 M-cell domain.

## Acceptance criteria

- Sequential observations affect later forecasts through the updated ensemble.
- Posterior covariance is not discarded between frames.
- With no observations, the cycle reproduces the open-loop ensemble forecast.
- With a near-perfect synthetic observation, spread contracts in the observed subspace without becoming negative or collapsing globally.
- Storage bounds hold and each analysis increment is auditable.
- Results are invariant to observation ordering within numerical tolerance.
- The implementation runs without materializing a dense full-domain cell-by-cell covariance.
